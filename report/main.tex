\begin{abstract}
Detailed multicompartment neuron models reproduce nonlinear dendritic,
synaptic, and somatic dynamics, but their computational cost limits repeated
experimentation and large-network simulation. Existing accelerators either
retain the original equations through hardware-aware solvers, reduce the
morphology, or learn an input--output approximation. These approaches are
effective for their intended targets, but they do not by themselves provide a
learned, restartable flow map over the complete internal state of the original
neuron. We introduce HayFlow, a developing surrogate of the Hay layer-5
pyramidal-cell model. HayFlow defines a causal one-millisecond transition over
all 642 instantiated segments, a rich boundary state, and realized synaptic
input. The project uses component-level, preregistered experiments to separate
information, representation, optimization, and recursive-exposure failures.
A frozen morphology-aware core followed by a direct-tree residual decoder
achieves an ensemble RMSE of \textbf{0.3308 mV} on a preregistered fresh
one-step test, versus 2.5763 mV for persistence. Under an independent
information-matched protocol, a compact HayFlow voltage bridge outperforms an
8,002-parameter Branch-ELM core for every paired seed, reducing median global,
somatic, and active-regime RMSE by 6.44\%, 34.94\%, and 8.92\%, respectively.
However, the one-step champion fails in closed loop. A separate recursive
prototype combining a passive Hines prior with four-millisecond pushforward
exposure reduces median eight-step RMSE from 18.162 to 14.720 mV and produces
no physical-voltage violations. It nevertheless fails a preregistered
cross-seed calibration veto and still receives teacher mechanism state during
rollout. The evidence therefore establishes an accurate one-step flow map, a
fair advantage over a compact phenomenological baseline, and a causally
supported route toward recursive stability, but not yet an autonomous
replacement for NEURON.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Problem and Importance}

Biophysically detailed neurons represent membrane voltage, ion-channel gates,
calcium dynamics, receptor-specific synaptic conductances, and axial current
over a branched morphology. The Hay layer-5 pyramidal-cell (L5PC) model is a
prominent example: it reproduces a broad range of dendritic and perisomatic
active properties and is commonly simulated with NEURON
\cite{hay2011,hines1997}. Such models are scientific instruments rather than
mere signal generators. Their internal state permits causal interventions,
snapshot restoration, branch-specific stimulation, and mechanistic analysis
of NMDA, calcium, backpropagating, and somatic events.

The same detail makes simulation expensive. Each numerical step couples many
compartments and mechanism variables, and biologically relevant trajectories
require thousands of small integration steps. This cost becomes prohibitive
when the model is embedded in a circuit, swept over input conditions, or used
inside an optimization loop. Accelerating one cell while preserving its
nonlinear computations is therefore important both for computational
neuroscience and for studying what a cortical neuron computes.

\subsection{Standard Solutions and the Remaining Gap}

Existing solutions fall into three families.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Exact numerical acceleration} retains the original equations
  and improves scheduling, vectorization, or hardware utilization.
  DeepDendrite parallelizes the Hines solve on GPUs and reports
  orders-of-magnitude acceleration without replacing the mechanistic model
  \cite{zhang2023}. This is preferable when exact state and arbitrary
  biophysical interventions are non-negotiable.
  \item \textbf{Analytical or phenomenological reduction} decreases the number
  of compartments or variables. Neuron\_Reduce preserves selected transfer
  impedances while mapping a detailed tree to a smaller one
  \cite{amsalem2020}. Reduced models are interpretable and fast, but their
  fidelity is tied to the preserved quantities and reduction assumptions.
  \item \textbf{Learned surrogates} fit the teacher's observable behavior.
  Temporal convolutional networks, CNN--LSTMs, and Expressive Leaky Memory
  neurons predict voltage and spikes much faster than detailed simulation
  \cite{beniaguev2021,olah2022,spieler2024}. Most target
  event-history-to-output behavior rather than the full causal state transition.
\end{enumerate}

The unresolved gap is a learned surrogate that is simultaneously
\emph{morphology preserving}, \emph{state complete enough for restart and
branching}, and \emph{stable under recursive use}. Low one-step voltage error
does not guarantee this: during deployment, a model consumes its own predicted
voltage and slow state, so small errors can alter voltage-dependent currents
and threshold events.

\subsection{Proposal and Design Rationale}

We propose HayFlow, a one-millisecond causal flow map over the complete
instantiated Hay morphology. Four arguments guide the design.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Expose causal state rather than infer it repeatedly from a
  finite voltage history.} Voltage alone is not Markovian for a
  conductance-based neuron. Mechanism, ionic, calcium, and synaptic states are
  recorded at each boundary.
  \item \textbf{Represent the dendritic tree explicitly.} Axial interaction is
  local on the morphology, while regenerative events depend on region and
  path. Tree features and a Hines-style solve are better aligned than an
  arbitrary compartment ordering.
  \item \textbf{Separate physical transport from learned nonlinear sources.}
  The cable solve is known and stable; the difficult local effective membrane
  source is learned. This focuses capacity where the teacher is nonlinear.
  \item \textbf{Test learnability before scale.} Small paired experiments,
  probes, path deletions, and mini scaling curves distinguish absent
  information, representation, optimization, and closed-loop exposure.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Hypotheses}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.2cm,style=nextline]
  \item[H1: Causal-state identifiability.]
  Rich boundary state and realized input identify the authentic one-millisecond
  transition substantially better than persistence or frozen H2.
  \item[H2: Morphology-aware representation.]
  Under identical numerical information and similar parameter budget, a
  morphology-aware HayFlow path should outperform Branch-ELM, especially at
  the soma and in active regimes.
  \item[H3: Non-compositionality of one-step accuracy.]
  Teacher-forced one-step selection will not necessarily remain accurate when
  the model is recursively applied to its own state.
  \item[H4: Structure-preserving prior.]
  A passive Hines prior should be safer recursively than an unconstrained
  learned transition, particularly in physical-voltage violations.
  \item[H5: Exposure alignment.]
  Pushforward exposure to model-generated boundaries should improve multi-step
  RMSE relative to teacher-boundary training.
  \item[H6: Event and stability objectives.]
  Ordered-event supervision and a passive-relative directional penalty may
  improve rollout; they are retained only if causal ablations show material gain.
\end{description}

\subsection{Contributions}

This report contributes: (i) a replay-validated causal teacher contract over
all 642 segments; (ii) a one-step checkpoint family evaluated on a sealed
fresh test; (iii) an information-matched Branch-ELM comparison; (iv) a causal
diagnosis of one-step composition failure; and (v) a paired factorial
experiment isolating pushforward exposure as the first materially successful
learned recursive intervention.

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Detailed Multicompartment Models}

Rall's cable-theoretic treatment established the basis for representing
dendrites as branched electrical structures. NEURON made empirically grounded
multicompartment neurons practical to construct and simulate
\cite{hines1997}. The Hay L5PC model combined reconstructed morphology with
active somatic, axonal, and dendritic mechanisms constrained by
electrophysiology \cite{hay2011}. These works are our mechanistic foundation:
HayFlow does not alter or retune the teacher.

Recent exact accelerators attack runtime rather than complexity. DeepDendrite
introduces dendritic hierarchical scheduling and GPU execution while retaining
the biophysical equations \cite{zhang2023}. Exact acceleration is preferable
when arbitrary channel parameters, unseen mechanisms, or exact trajectories
must change at inference. HayFlow targets repeated evaluation within a fixed
teacher family and state contract.

\subsection{Morphological and Dynamical Reduction}

Neuron\_Reduce maps synapses and active channels to a smaller tree while
preserving selected transfer impedances and reports substantial speedups on
nonlinear neurons \cite{amsalem2020}. It is strong when retained somatic and
dendritic behaviors are specified in advance. We keep all 642 segments so that
learned-dynamics error is not confounded with morphology-reduction error.
Reduction remains a later, separately measurable compression stage.

\subsection{Learned Neuron Surrogates}

Beniaguev, Segev, and London showed that a temporal convolutional network can
approximate the spike-level input--output transformation of a cortical neuron
\cite{beniaguev2021}. Ol\'ah, Pedersen, and Rowan used CNN--LSTMs for several
multicompartment cells and demonstrated large circuit-level runtime gains
\cite{olah2022}. Their principal contract is presynaptic history to
voltage/spikes rather than complete state evolution.

Spieler et al. introduced ELM, combining filtered synaptic traces, multiple
leaky memories, nonlinear integration, and a compact readout
\cite{spieler2024}. Branch-ELM encodes dendritic grouping and fits NeuronIO with
fewer than ten thousand parameters. It is our closest compact learned baseline.
HayFlow differs by receiving a rich causal boundary state, preserving all 642
segment outputs, and targeting the next internal state. Published ELM and
HayFlow errors are not directly comparable; we therefore run a separate
information-matched experiment.

\subsection{Learned Physical Simulators and Rollout Stability}

Graph Networks and MeshGraphNets model physical systems as interacting entities
\cite{sanchez2020,pfaff2021}; Fourier neural operators learn maps between
function spaces \cite{li2021}. These motivate topology-aware learning, but a
neuron adds heterogeneous mechanisms, receptor state, stiffness, and threshold
events. Scheduled sampling addresses teacher-forcing exposure mismatch
\cite{bengio2015}. Brandstetter et al. cast autoregressive PDE stability as
domain adaptation and introduced pushforward training \cite{brandstetter2022}.
HayFlow tests this intervention causally on a neuronal state contract.

\section{Background and Problem Formulation}

For segment $i$, compartmental dynamics can be written schematically as
\begin{equation}
 C_i\frac{dV_i}{dt}=I_{\mathrm{axial},i}(V)
 +I_{\mathrm{ion},i}(V_i,z_i,c_i)
 +I_{\mathrm{syn},i}(V_i,r_i,U)+I_{\mathrm{ext},i},
 \label{eq:cable}
\end{equation}
with channel states $z_i$, concentrations $c_i$, and synaptic state $r_i$.
Their additional kinetics imply that voltage alone is not closed: equal
voltage with different $(z,c,r)$ can have different futures.

Let $S_t$ be the complete recorded teacher state at a millisecond boundary and
$U_{[t,t+1)}$ the realized input during the interval. The target is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal F_{1\,\mathrm{ms}}:(S_t,U_{[t,t+1)})\mapsto
 (S_{t+1},V_{[t,t+1]},E_{[t,t+1)}),
 \label{eq:flowmap}
\end{equation}
where $V$ is a probe microtrace and $E$ includes somatic, axonal,
backpropagating, calcium, and NMDA events. $S_t$ contains voltage at 642
segments, accessible NMODL \texttt{STATE} variables, calcium/ions, synaptic
state, morphology/mechanism masks, and replay metadata. $U$ contains ordered
events, timestamps, synapse identity, receptor-aware realized release, and
external current. Realized release is causal because the authentic synaptic
front end determines it before membrane integration.

One-step evaluation uses $\widehat S_{t+1}=\widehat{\mathcal F}(S_t,U_t)$.
Autonomous rollout instead uses
\begin{equation}
 \widehat S_{t+k+1}=\widehat{\mathcal F}(\widehat S_{t+k},U_{t+k}).
 \label{eq:rollout}
\end{equation}
New input does not reset errors stored in voltage, calcium, gates, or synaptic
tails. One-step and rollout candidates therefore require separate gates.

Components are tested before composition; seeds, initialization, minibatches,

checkpoint ladders reveal mini scaling laws; and fresh outcomes never select a

\subsection{Model A: \modela}

Model A is a frozen HayFlow-Hines H2 core plus a direct-tree residual decoder.
H2 preserves the 642-segment morphology, uses one physical Hines solve per
macro-step, and produces a voltage proposal. The decoder receives 226 fixed
multiscale tree features and a learned 16-dimensional segment embedding. A
shared SiLU MLP $242\rightarrow96\rightarrow96\rightarrow1$ predicts
\begin{equation}
 \widehat V_{t+1,i}=V^{\mathrm{H2}}_{t+1,i}
 +120\tanh(g_\theta(\phi_{t,i},e_i)/120).
 \label{eq:modela}
\end{equation}
It has 43,009 trainable parameters; frozen H2 is additional. Tree features
expose multiscale context, embeddings allow local specialization, sharing
avoids 642 independent models, and the residual preserves a known baseline.

\subsection{Model B: \modelb}

Model B preserves the passive cable solve and learns an effective membrane
source:
\begin{equation}
 A_{\mathrm{passive}}(S_t)\widehat V_{t+1}
 =b_{\mathrm{passive}}(S_t,U_t)+\widehat q_\theta(S_t,U_t).
 \label{eq:modelb}
\end{equation}
The Hines-structured matrix propagates axial/passive effects; a
12,972-parameter cell predicts the nonlinear source. Four-millisecond
pushforward exposure generates intermediate predicted states before later
losses are evaluated. This changes the training distribution without using
future teacher state as input.

The registered $2\times2\times2$ experiment crosses: ordered-event auxiliary
on/off; teacher-boundary versus 4 ms pushforward exposure; and
passive-relative directional penalty on/off. All arms share initialization,
windows, minibatches, and budget.

\section{Experimental Setup}

\subsection{Teacher, Data, and Splits}

The teacher is the original Hay L5PC implementation from
\texttt{neuron\_as\_deep\_net}, pinned and audited before generation. The base
diagnostic store contains 29,240 one-millisecond transitions and a
validation-only top-up contains 640, yielding 29,880 logical transitions
without physically merging the HDF5 stores. Splits are separated by seed and
protocol, not by random windows from one trajectory.

The one-step fresh test is distinct: 32 causal boundary pairs, 64 episodes,
and 768 transitions. It was generated from a sealed plan only after Model A
was frozen. Exhaustive replay reached $3.815\times10^{-6}$ maximum discrepancy,
below the $10^{-5}$ tolerance.

The matched Branch-ELM comparison uses train-derived roles: 1,024 fit, 256
calibration, and 256 disjoint development transitions. The 06b-r factorial
uses bounded event-balanced windows, disjoint calibration halves for
checkpoint and arm decisions, and development only afterward. It never opens
validation, test, or fresh-test state.

In the matched comparison, both models receive the same 76-value per-segment

and cannot influence voltage.

\subsection{Parameter Tuning and Selection}

Fresh data are never used for tuning. Model A's architecture was fixed before
refit; only representation statistics, weights, and stopping epoch were fitted
internally. A seed passed only if it improved internal calibration over H2 by
at least 10\%, improved frozen development over the better of H2 and
persistence by at least 5\%, retained branch contrast in $[0.5,2.0]$, and did
not exceed H2 maximum segment error. Two of three seeds were required.

For Model B, factors and thresholds were preregistered. The 0/40/120/240
checkpoint ladder is a mini scaling curve. Calibration half A selects an epoch;
half B applies a no-seed-regression veto. Development cannot reverse that
decision. This is deliberately stricter than choosing the visually best run.

\subsection{Baselines and Metrics}

One-step baselines are persistence and frozen H2; the compact architecture
competitor is Branch-ELM. Recursive references are support-matched passive
Hines and frozen 06b-q. Metrics include global, soma, and active-regime RMSE;
maximum segment error; branching retention; horizon RMSE; mean drift; physical
violations outside $[-120,80]$ mV; marginal factor effects; and path-deletion
effects.

Runtime acceleration is a final objective, but a controlled common-hardware
teacher-versus-surrogate benchmark has not been registered. We therefore do
not claim speedup. Interpretability is assessed structurally through explicit
morphology, physical priors, path deletion, and factor ablation rather than an
unvalidated scalar score.

\subsection{Implementation and Reproducibility}

Experiments use PyTorch on CUDA-enabled Kaggle runtimes. Every archive contains
the code revision, configuration, checkpoints, dataset hashes, and an indexed
SHA-256 manifest. The GPU model was not consistently recorded and is not
claimed. Code and records are at \url{https://github.com/Zagred47/giada}.
Central revisions are \texttt{c1b48fe} (05j-o), \texttt{a26ee9e} (matched
comparison), and \texttt{cbd7be5} (06b-r).

strongly supported: one-step RMSE alone is not a deployment criterion.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Scientific Progress and Method Advantages}

Three independent gains are established. The teacher contract converts an
ambiguous history-to-output problem into a replayable causal transition. The
direct-tree decoder shows this one-step map is accurately learnable on sealed
support. Pushforward exposure then reduces recursive error below a physical
baseline without violations, proving that the prior 46.94 mV failure was not
an unavoidable learned-model limit.

The Branch-ELM result is narrower: with information equalized,
morphology-aware features confer a consistent advantage, especially at the
soma. It does not imply full Model A dominates ELM universally. ELM is smaller
and naturally recurrent; HayFlow exposes more teacher state and targets a
different deployment contract.

\subsection{When HayFlow Should and Should Not Be Preferred}

HayFlow should eventually be preferred when complete local voltage, causal
restart, counterfactual branching, and a differentiable learned core are
required. Explicit state, topology, and factorized physics make its failures
more diagnosable than a black-box event-to-soma model.

It should not currently replace NEURON for exact mechanisms or unseen parameter
interventions. DeepDendrite is preferable when exact acceleration and hardware
are available; Neuron\_Reduce when an interpretable reduced morphology is
acceptable; Branch-ELM or CNN--LSTM when only fast voltage/spikes are needed.
These conditions define scope rather than hidden caveats.

\subsection{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Data are diagnostic and event enriched, not yet at NeuronIO scale or
  naturalistic coverage.
  \item The 0.3308 mV result is one-step only.
  \item The 14.720 mV result is development evidence and fails calibration
  promotion.
  \item Model B receives teacher mechanism state during rollout and is not
  state closed.
  \item The ELM comparison is development-only and has a modest parameter
  mismatch.
  \item Common-hardware speed, memory, and energy have not been measured.
  \item Rapid firing, event timing, long plateaus, longer horizons, unseen
  protocols, networks, and other morphologies remain unvalidated.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}

HayFlow combines a fresh-tested 0.3308 mV one-step family with a separate
pushforward recursive prototype improving 8 ms error by 18.95\% over passive
Hines. Under identical causal input, its compact voltage bridge improves on
Branch-ELM by 6.44\% globally and 34.94\% at the soma. The main result is not a
finished replacement for NEURON: rich causal state makes the one-step Hay
transition accurately learnable, while stable composition requires explicit
exposure to model-generated boundaries.

Next work will retain passive physics, compact causal input, and pushforward
exposure while removing inert branches. A candidate must jointly predict
mechanism state, use nested cross-seed/horizon calibration, and pass
event-positive and longer rollouts before a new sealed test. Only after state
closure should the project scale data, benchmark speed/memory, test other
morphologies, and evaluate multi-neuron deployment.
